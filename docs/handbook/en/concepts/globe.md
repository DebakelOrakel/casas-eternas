A sphere world is a quarter of Earth: 20 000 km round the equator, about
127 million km² of surface. It has two poles and wraps only from east to
west. Its axis leans by the axial tilt, so the sun stands overhead on the
equator only twice a year.

## Looking at it {#view}

- **Drag** turns the globe: the point you grab stays under the pointer.
  Let go while you move, and it turns on a little.
- **Scroll** zooms in and out. Zoomed out, the globe fills most of the
  screen; zoomed in, you look at a region.
- **Reset view** (bottom left) shows the whole globe again, unturned.
- **Rotate globe** turns it slowly about its axis. It turns faster on a
  short day and slower on a long one.
- **Tilt view** looks across the surface toward the horizon instead of
  straight down.

The curved scale on the right of the globe gives the distance along its
surface at the place you look at. On the left, a scale shows the
temperature by latitude; in the World step the equator and the two polar
circles show as bands on the globe.
