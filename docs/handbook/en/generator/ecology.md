What the land yields. From relief, rock, climate and water follow where
farming is possible, where there are fish, game and pasture, where timber
grows, where salt, tool stone and ores lie. Together the food sources say
how many people a place can carry.

## What this step does {#does}

- It derives thirteen resources from the finished world, in four groups:
  subsistence, material, metals and prestige.
- It combines the food sources into the carrying capacity.
- It shows the resources that change with the seasons for each month.

The map always shows one resource; you pick it in the left column.

## Concepts {#concepts}

### Carrying capacity {#resource.carryingCapacity}

How many people a place can feed. It comes from arable land, fish, game
and pasture; salt helps a little, because it keeps food. The sources
complement each other, but each one more adds less: a place with
everything is rich, but not four times as rich as one with one good
source. Gold and ores feed nobody.

### Three roles {#roles}

Subsistence decides how many people can live. Material decides what they
can build and make: timber, salt, tool stone and metals. Prestige feeds
nobody, but it makes power and trade: gold, silver and gems.

## Resources {#resources}

### Arable land {#resource.arable}

Soil and climate fit for farming: months without frost, flat land, water
on the fields and rain one can rely on.

### Fish {#resource.fish}

Sea fishing, richest above cool upwelling and on the shelves; and fish
from rivers and lakes.

### Game and forage {#resource.game}

Wild animals to hunt and plants to gather. Most where the land grows much
and different biomes meet.

### Pasture {#resource.pasture}

Grazing for herds, by biome. In dry land only as far as a herd finds
water.

### Timber {#resource.timber}

Usable wood: much in forests, little in open or cold land.

### Salt {#resource.salt}

Evaporation salt, strongest on warm, dry coasts and on the salt flats of
dry basins.

### Tool stone {#resource.toolStone}

Hard stone that can be knapped into tools: obsidian near volcanoes, flint
scattered across flat land.

### Copper {#resource.copper}

Copper ore, bound to the volcanoes of island arcs.

### Tin {#resource.tin}

Tin ore from collision belts: rare, and wanted for bronze.

### Iron {#resource.iron}

Iron ore, the most widespread of the metals: in old cratons and as bog
iron.

### Gold {#resource.gold}

Enriched near volcanic arcs and in old cratons.

### Silver {#resource.silver}

From hot solutions near volcanic arcs.

### Gems {#resource.gems}

From mountain belts whose rock was changed by pressure and heat, and from
the gravels of dry lands.

## Parameters {#parameters}

Which slider shows depends on the resource you picked.

### Abundance {#abundance}

For a single resource: how much of it there is, 50 % to 200 %. If you
change arable land, fish, game, pasture or salt, the carrying capacity
changes with it.

### Carrying capacity {#generator.panel.ecology.carryingCapacity}

For the carrying capacity: how many people the land can carry in all,
50 % to 200 %. It changes only the level, not where it is rich and where
poor.

### Concentration {#generator.panel.ecology.concentration}

Whether the carrying capacity spreads evenly or gathers into rich
pockets, −100 to 100. The total stays the same.

### Provinces {#generator.panel.ecology.provinces}

How strongly fertile provinces around volcanoes stand out, 0 to 100
(default 45). Volcanic soils are rich; this slider says how much.

## Controls {#controls}

### Month {#generator.ecology.month}

Which month the map shows. Arable land, fish, game, pasture and salt
change with the seasons; timber, stone and metals are yearly values.

### Reset {#generator.action.resetEcology}

Puts all the sliders of this step back to their defaults.
